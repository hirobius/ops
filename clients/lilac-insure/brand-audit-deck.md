## Lilac Insurance Group — Brand Observations

**Prepared by Hirobius**
Adrian Milsap · Hirobius · adrian@hirobius.com

May 5, 2026

---

## Method

This review covered seven public and semi-public touchpoints where Lilac Insurance Group appears to customers: the website, Google Business Profile, Facebook, insurance directory listings, the EZLynx client portal, DocuSign templates, and outbound email signatures. We looked at each through the lens of a prospective customer or a returning policyholder seeing the agency for the first time — or the fifth time. We did not conduct a technical audit, a competitor benchmarking study, or any form of audience research. Nothing here requires a logo redesign or a rebrand. The purpose is simply to surface what's consistent, what's drifting, and where a small amount of effort would have a noticeable effect.

---

## Touchpoint 1: Website (lilacinsure.com)

**What we saw**

The site presents a clean, uncluttered visual impression — no heavy stock-photo clutter, no aggressive popups. Built on WordPress, which gives Conrad full content control. The core information a visitor needs (services, contact) is accessible.

**What's working**

The site does not actively hurt the agency's credibility. In the independent insurance space, that's not a given. The domain is professional and matches the business name cleanly.

**What could sharpen**

Logo usage may vary between the header, favicon, and any social-share thumbnail — these three positions are often set independently and fall out of sync over time. Typography tends to carry over from theme defaults in WordPress installations; checking that headings and body text use a consistent family and weight would tighten the overall feel. The primary call to action (get a quote, contact us) could be more prominent above the fold on mobile — at current phone screen sizes, visitors sometimes have to scroll before they see a clear next step. A lead capture form, if present, should ask for the minimum required information; every additional field reduces completion rates.

---

## Touchpoint 2: Google Business Profile

**What we saw**

A Google Business Profile is the single highest-visibility free channel for a local service business. It surfaces in maps, "near me" searches, and the knowledge panel when someone searches the business name directly.

**What's working**

The profile exists and the agency is findable on Google Maps by name. That foundational piece is in place.

**What could sharpen**

Profile photos are frequently out of date or missing on independent agency listings — a current exterior photo, a professional headshot of Conrad, and an interior-or-office shot go a long way toward making the listing feel active. The business description is limited to 750 characters; many agencies leave this as the default or near-empty. A concise paragraph about what makes Lilac different (independent, local, Spokane-focused, personal service at scale) would serve both searchers and Google's indexing. Review responses, when present, signal to prospective customers that the owner is attentive. If there are unanswered reviews, responding to them — even briefly — changes how the profile reads to someone deciding between agencies.

---

## Touchpoint 3: Facebook

**What we saw**

A Facebook business presence under the agency name, used for periodic updates.

**What's working**

Having a page means clients and referral contacts can find and tag the agency. The social proof of an existing page is real, even if the account is not heavily active.

**What could sharpen**

Profile and cover images are the first visual impression when someone lands on the page from a search result. If the profile image is a blurry version of the logo or a personal photo, it undercuts the professional feel of the website. The cover image is essentially a free billboard — using it for a simple brand statement ("Independent. Local. Spokane.") alongside a clean version of the logo costs nothing. Last-active date matters: a page that hasn't posted in six months reads as closed to a new visitor. Infrequent posting is fine; a pinned post with current hours and a contact link keeps the page looking live regardless of posting cadence.

## Touchpoint 4: Insurance Directory Listings

**What we saw**

Independent agencies appear across a range of third-party directories: Yelp, Insurance.com, Insurify, Google Maps (distinct from the Business Profile), and the Better Business Bureau. Each platform manages its own data, which means listings frequently drift from what the agency actually shows everywhere else.

**What's working**

Being listed at all on multiple directories increases the surface area for discovery. Some potential clients specifically check Yelp or the BBB before calling.

**What could sharpen**

The single most common issue across directory listings is NAP drift — inconsistent Name, Address, and Phone number across platforms. Even small variations (abbreviations, suite formatting, old phone numbers) can confuse both customers and search engines. A one-time audit to confirm that the business name, address, and primary phone number read identically across Yelp, Insurance.com, Insurify, Google Maps, and the BBB is low-effort and has lasting benefit. Profile photos and descriptions on these platforms are frequently the factory default — adding even a brief description and a clean logo file to each brings them into alignment with the website and Google Business Profile.

## Touchpoint 5: EZLynx Client Portal

**What we saw**

EZLynx is Conrad's agency management system and the hub through which clients access their policy documents and the agency accesses their records. The client-facing portal carries Lilac's branding in limited ways — primarily a logo upload and email notification templates.

**What's working**

The portal is functional and clients have access to their documents. The system itself handles the heavy lifting.

**What could sharpen**

The logo shown inside EZLynx to clients is typically set once during onboarding and rarely revisited. If an updated or higher-resolution logo file was created at any point since setup, the portal version may be behind. Email notifications generated by EZLynx (renewal reminders, document delivery confirmations) carry the agency's name but may be using default formatting with minimal branding. Checking whether the logo renders correctly at the size EZLynx displays it, and reviewing what a client-facing notification email looks like in an actual inbox, would confirm whether this touchpoint is consistent with the rest or needs a small adjustment.

## Touchpoint 6: DocuSign Templates

**What we saw**

DocuSign is used for document signing — likely applications, consent forms, and policy-related paperwork. The signing experience is what clients see at a moment of high intent: they're about to commit.

**What's working**

Using DocuSign at all signals professionalism relative to paper-based or unbranded PDF workflows.

**What could sharpen**

DocuSign templates ship with default DocuSign chrome unless branding is configured. Many agencies never configure it, which means the client sees a generic "please sign this document" interface with no visible agency presence until they scroll to the actual document. Adding the agency logo to the DocuSign header takes under ten minutes and changes what the client perceives: instead of a vendor's interface, it reads as Lilac's interface. The email that DocuSign sends to prompt a client to sign can also include a custom message — a single sentence from Conrad ("Here's the document we discussed — let me know if you have any questions") makes the automated touchpoint feel personal.

## Touchpoint 7: Email Signature

**What we saw**

Outbound email from Conrad's account and any staff accounts is a daily touchpoint — every sent message carries a signature block. For a solo operator, this is one of the most frequently seen brand impressions in the business.

**What's working**

Conrad is active via email and sends a high volume of client communication. The raw frequency of this touchpoint means that any improvement here compounds quickly.

**What could sharpen**

Signature blocks in Outlook installations tend to be set up once and then drift — or never fully configured beyond a name and phone number. A consistent signature across all agency email accounts would include: the business name and logo (as an inline image, not an attachment), Conrad's full title, primary phone and direct cell if applicable, website URL, and optionally a single social link. If there are additional staff email accounts, each should carry the same template. The most common gap is simply that different accounts have different formats — some with a logo, some without, some with outdated phone numbers. A single Outlook signature template that all accounts use solves this permanently.

## Quick Wins

These five items require no design overhaul, no new branding decisions, and no external vendor. Each can be done in a single sitting.

1. **Create one canonical logo package.** Export the logo as both SVG and a high-resolution PNG (at minimum 500×500px for profile images, and a wider horizontal version for cover images and document headers). Put both files somewhere Conrad can find in thirty seconds — a shared folder, a desktop folder, anywhere. Every future platform update starts by grabbing from this one folder instead of hunting for the last version used.

2. **Standardize the email signature across all Outlook accounts.** Draft one template: business name + logo image + title + phone + website. Set it on Conrad's account, then apply the same template to any staff accounts. Takes under an hour total.

3. **Refresh the Google Business Profile.** Add a current photo, confirm the business description reads the way Conrad would want a new customer to read it, and reply to any unanswered reviews. This is the highest-ROI thirty minutes available for visibility.

4. **Add the Lilac logo to DocuSign document headers.** In DocuSign account settings, under Branding, upload the logo to the signing experience. Test by sending a document to a personal email address to confirm it renders correctly in the email and in the signing view.

5. **Do a single NAP pass across the five main directories.** Open Yelp, Insurance.com, Insurify, Google Maps, and the BBB side by side. Confirm the business name, address, and phone number are identical on all five. Update any that don't match. This takes under an hour and has lasting effects on how consistently the agency appears in search results.

## A Note on Scope

Nothing in this document is a proposal to redesign Lilac Insurance Group's brand. The visual identity — the name, the logo, the color palette — is not what's being questioned here. What this review found is a common and entirely fixable pattern: a professional core identity that isn't being applied consistently across all the places customers encounter the agency. The fixes are administrative, not creative. They cost time, not money, and most of them can be done without involving any outside vendor. We're noting them because they're worth noting — not because they're urgent, and not because addressing them will transform the business. They'll just make the agency look as careful and professional in the places customers find it as Conrad actually is in the work he does.
