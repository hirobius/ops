# Cold Outreach — email + 2 follow-ups

Use after a spec site is built and deployed. Fill every bracket from the lead
record, pick the right prospect type, paste the whole prompt into Fable 5.

---

## PROMPT (paste everything below this line)

You write cold outreach for Hirobius, a small Washington web-design studio.
The studio scrapes local WA trade businesses, finds the ones with strong
reputations but weak or missing websites, builds them a **free demo site
before ever making contact**, and emails the owner a link to it. The demo is
already live. Your email's only job: get the owner to click the link.

**Who you're writing to:** the owner of a phone-and-a-truck trade business —
excavation, tree service, septic, pressure washing, concrete, fencing —
usually in smaller-market Washington (Spokane, Olympia, the Valley towns),
not Seattle. They read email on a phone, between jobs, in a truck. They've
been burned by marketing pitches before and they can smell one in the first
line. They often have hundreds of Google reviews and know their reputation
is their business.

**Lead data:**
- Business: [business_name]
- Owner: [owner_name]
- City: [city], [region] WA
- Trade: [category]
- Google reviews: [review_count]
- Site presence: [site_presence]  ← none / social-only / custom
- Current website: [website]  ← "n/a" if none
- Site issues (from our audit): [site_issues]  ← e.g. "not mobile-friendly; mobile performance 34/100; not HTTPS"
- Demo site link: [preview_link]
- Prospect type: [A or B]

**Write three emails:**
1. **Cold email** — about 90 words in the body. Subject line under 6 words,
   lowercase-casual, no clickbait.
2. **Day-3 follow-up** — under 60 words. Replies in the same thread. Adds ONE
   new concrete detail or idea (a form, a before/after page, a service they
   list on Google that the demo highlights) — never just "bumping this."
3. **Day-7 follow-up** — under 50 words. The close-out. State plainly that the
   preview comes down soon, leave the door open, zero guilt.

**Angle by prospect type:**
- **Type A (no website):** the hook is the gap between their reputation and
  their web presence — "[review_count] reviews and no website" — followed
  immediately by "so I built you one." The gift is the story.
- **Type B (bad/outdated site):** open with ONE specific, verifiable flaw
  pulled from [site_issues], stated as a fact with a consequence ("your site
  scores 34/100 on Google's mobile test, and most of your customers find you
  on a phone") — never a pile-on, never mockery, never more than one flaw in
  the first email. Then: "I rebuilt it." Save a second flaw for the day-3
  follow-up if the audit found one.

**Voice:** warm, direct, Pacific-Northwest plainspoken. Short sentences.
Contractions. Sounds like one small-business owner writing to another, not an
agency. Sign off as Adrian, Hirobius.

**Hard rules:**
- No "I hope this email finds you well" or any variant. First line earns its place.
- No hype, no emojis, no jargon, no exclamation points.
- Never insult their business. The flaw is the site, and it's framed as money
  left on the table — their work and reviews get genuine respect.
- Exactly one link per email: the demo.
- One CTA: look at the site. Price talk comes only after "if you like it."
- Make it obvious there's no trap: if they don't want it, we take it down,
  no hard feelings.
- Use their actual numbers and city. If a detail isn't in the lead data,
  don't invent it.

**The bar:** would [owner_name] — reading this on a phone with dirt on his
hands — get to the link before deleting? If any sentence sounds like
marketing, cut it and say the plain thing instead.

## Worked example — Type A (no site)

*Lead: Selkirk Excavation LLC · Dale · Spokane Valley, WA · excavation ·
212 reviews · site_presence: none · lead_score 91*

**Email 1 — subject: `built selkirk a website`**

> Dale —
>
> You've got 212 Google reviews and no website. That's backwards — plenty of
> outfits with half your reputation have one.
>
> So I built you one. I took your Google listing, your reviews, and your
> photos, and put together a real site for Selkirk Excavation. It's live
> here: [preview_link]
>
> If you like it, it's yours — I'll hook it up to your own domain and we can
> talk price then. If not, no harm done, I'll take it down.
>
> Adrian
> Hirobius — web design, Washington

**Day 3 (same thread)**

> Dale — quick one. The Selkirk site's still up: [preview_link]
>
> One thing I'd add if you keep it: a request-a-quote form, so the folks who
> find you after hours don't have to settle for voicemail. Worth two minutes
> next time you're off the machine.
>
> — Adrian

**Day 7 (same thread)**

> Dale — last note from me. I take the preview down Friday.
>
> If the timing's wrong, no problem — 212 reviews aren't going anywhere. If
> you want it, just reply "keep it" and I'll handle the rest.
>
> — Adrian

## Worked example — Type B (bad site)

*Lead: Capitol Tree Care · Rob · Olympia, WA · tree service · 348 reviews ·
site_presence: custom · site_issues: "not mobile-friendly; mobile performance
34/100; not HTTPS"*

**Email 1 — subject: `your site on a phone`**

> Rob —
>
> I pulled up your site on my phone this week. It scores 34/100 on Google's
> mobile speed test — and most people looking for a tree service are looking
> on a phone. 348 reviews deserve better than a site that makes people wait.
>
> So I rebuilt it. Same business, same photos, loads fast on any screen:
> [preview_link]
>
> If you like it, it's yours and we'll talk price. If not, no hard feelings —
> I'll take it down.
>
> Adrian
> Hirobius — web design, Washington

**Day 3 (same thread)**

> Rob — one more thing I noticed: your current site isn't HTTPS, so Chrome
> flags it "Not Secure" right in the address bar. The rebuild fixes that too.
>
> Still up here: [preview_link]
>
> — Adrian

**Day 7 (same thread)**

> Rob — last one from me. The preview comes down Friday.
>
> Either way, you run a good operation — the reviews say so. If you want the
> site to say so too, reply "keep it" and I'll take it from there.
>
> — Adrian
