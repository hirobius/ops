# Spec-Site Copy — hero, services, About

Use when building the free demo site for a scraped lead. All inputs come from
their Google Business Profile — we've never spoken to this owner. The copy
has to feel like *they* wrote it, because the first person to judge it is the
owner themselves, holding their phone, deciding whether this studio gets them.

---

## PROMPT (paste everything below this line)

You write website copy for Hirobius, a Washington web-design studio that
builds free demo sites for local trade businesses, then shows the owner.

**The audience is double:** (1) the homeowner in [city] who lands on this
page needing [trade] work — they should want to call within ten seconds;
(2) the business owner seeing their own demo for the first time — they should
think "that's us, that's exactly us," not "that's a template with my name
dropped in."

**Business data (from their Google profile):**
- Business: [business_name]
- Trade: [category]
- City / area: [city], [region] WA — service area: [service_area]
- Google reviews: [review_count] (avg [avg_rating] if known)
- Services listed: [services — comma-separated, pick the top 3]
- Owner: [owner_name] (if known)
- Years in business / anything from their profile description: [profile_notes]

**Write:**
1. **Hero headline** — 10 words max. Says what they do and where. Plain and
   confident, not a slogan. A stranger should know the trade and the town
   from the headline alone.
2. **Subhead** — 1–2 sentences. Work in the review count as proof, naturally.
3. **Three service blurbs** — one per service, 40–60 words each, with a
   4-6-word heading. Each blurb answers: what is it, when do you need it,
   why this crew. At least one blurb should touch a real local condition —
   Spokane freeze-thaw and rocky ground, Olympia's wet winters and moss,
   basalt, clay, storm season — whatever genuinely fits this trade and place.
4. **About paragraph** — 80–120 words, first person plural, in the owner's
   voice. Grounded in what we actually know (town, years, reviews, trade).
   No biography we can't back up.

**Voice:** the owner's own voice — plainspoken, proud of the work, direct
about what they do and don't do. Short sentences. Contractions. Warm but not
folksy-performative.

**Hard rules:**
- Banned phrases and their cousins: "your trusted partner," "we pride
  ourselves," "look no further," "top-notch," "solutions," "exceeding
  expectations," "committed to excellence." If it could be pasted onto any
  contractor site in America, it fails.
- Every section must contain at least one detail specific to THIS business —
  the town, the trade's real problems, the review count, a service they
  actually list. No invented awards, staff, or history.
- Local means real: name the actual towns and conditions of [service_area],
  not "the greater area."
- Homeowner language, not trade jargon: "the pipe between your house and the
  tank," not "lateral line" — unless the jargon is what customers search.

**The bar:** if you swapped in a competitor's name, the copy should break.
If it still reads fine, it's template copy — rewrite it.

## Worked example

*Input: Ponderosa Septic & Drain · septic services · Spokane, WA · service
area: Spokane, Spokane Valley, Cheney, Mead, Deer Park · 187 reviews, 4.9 ·
services: septic pumping, septic inspections, drainfield repair · family-run,
since 2009*

**Hero headline**

> Septic pumping, inspections, and repair — Spokane County.

**Subhead**

> Family-run since 2009, with 187 five-star reviews and a truck that shows up
> when we say it will. From Cheney to Deer Park, we keep your septic system
> out of sight and out of mind.

**Service blurbs**

> **Septic pumping, on schedule**
> Most tanks around Spokane need pumping every three to five years — sooner
> with a big household. We pump the tank, check the baffles and lids while
> we're in there, and tell you straight what we found. No upsell, no scare
> talk. Just a clean tank and a date for next time.

> **Inspections that hold up**
> Buying or selling a home on septic? Lenders and buyers want more than a
> glance at the lid. We run a full inspection — tank, lines, drainfield —
> and hand you a written report the same week. In a market that moves fast,
> we work around your closing date, not the other way around.

> **Drainfield repair in tough ground**
> Spokane ground is honest about its problems: basalt close to the surface,
> freeze-thaw every winter, soil that drains great in one yard and barely at
> all next door. When a drainfield fails, we find out why before we dig.
> Sometimes it's a repair, not a replacement — and we'll tell you which.

**About**

> We started Ponderosa Septic & Drain in 2009 with one truck and a simple
> rule: treat every tank like it's behind our own house. Fifteen years later
> we're still family-run, still based right here in Spokane, and still
> answering our own phone. We work everywhere from Cheney to Mead to Deer
> Park, and most of our new customers come from the old ones — that's what
> 187 five-star reviews will do. Septic isn't glamorous work, but it's work
> that matters, and we do it right the first time.
