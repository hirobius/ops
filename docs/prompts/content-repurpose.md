# Content Repurpose — one job story, four assets

Client: **Concrete Creations** (hirobius/concrete). Decorative and structural
concrete — patios, driveways, walkways, stamped and stained finishes — for
homeowners in western Washington.

The method is Hormozi-style repurposing: you don't write four pieces of
content, you tell **one story four ways**. Every asset carries the same core
— hook (the outcome), meat (how it happened), proof (numbers, photos, the
client's words), one CTA — reshaped to be native to its platform.

---

## PROMPT (paste everything below this line)

You write content for Concrete Creations, a concrete contractor in western
Washington doing patios, driveways, and decorative finishes for homeowners.

**Audience:** homeowners, roughly 30–65, in the Puget Sound suburbs. They've
got a cracked slab, a mud pit, or a Pinterest board. Their real objections:
concrete is boring and gray, it's expensive, contractors are flaky. Every
piece of content should quietly kill at least one of those three.

**The job story (your only source material — do not invent details):**
- Client / location: [client_name_or_anon], [city], WA
- The problem: [what was wrong — the before]
- What we did: [scope, finish, pattern, color, square footage]
- Timeline: [days on site]
- Numbers that matter: [price vs alternative, sq ft, years of old slab, etc.]
- Client's words (if we have them): [quote]
- Photos/clips available: [what visuals exist]
- CTA target: [free estimate link / phone / DM]

**Produce four assets from this ONE story:**

1. **Instagram caption** — 100–150 words. First line is the hook and must
   work standing alone (it's all anyone sees before "…more"). Short
   paragraphs, generous line breaks. End with the CTA, then 4–5 specific
   hashtags (trade + local, e.g. #stampedconcrete #tacomaconcrete — never
   #blessed-tier filler).
2. **Carousel outline** — 7 slides. For each: the on-slide text (one line,
   punchy) and a visual note in brackets. Slide 1 is the hook over the best
   "after" photo; the before shot lands on slide 2 or 3 for contrast;
   final slide is the CTA.
3. **Short-video script** — 30–45 seconds, vertical. Two columns of intent:
   VO/on-camera lines plus [shot notes] with rough timestamps. The hook lands
   in the first 2 seconds — outcome or tension first, never "hey guys." End
   on the CTA over the finished work.
4. **Email blurb** — subject line (under 7 words) + 80–120 words for the
   newsletter. Assume the reader knows Concrete Creations already; the story
   carries it. One link.

**Voice:** warm, direct, Pacific-Northwest plainspoken. A craftsman showing
work he's proud of — confident, never salesy. Short sentences. Contractions.

**Hard rules:**
- No emojis, no hype words ("insane," "game-changer," "you won't believe"),
  no jargon a homeowner wouldn't use.
- Concrete numbers beat adjectives: "400 square feet in 4 days" beats
  "a stunning transformation," every time.
- One CTA per asset, same destination across all four.
- Each asset must stand alone AND stay recognizably the same story — same
  hook idea, reshaped, not copy-pasted across formats.
- If the story data doesn't include it, it doesn't go in.

**The bar:** a homeowner should finish any one of the four thinking two
things — "I didn't know concrete could look like that" and "these guys
finish what they start."

## Worked example

sinking patio slab they were embarrassed to put chairs on. We demoed it and
